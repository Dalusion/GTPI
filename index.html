03 / The methodology

Show your work.

GTPI is an exploratory index. Its formula is a choice, not a universal measure of government efficiency.

2026 preview score

First put human development and government effectiveness on comparable 0–100 scales, then combine them using adjustable weights. Divide by the tax-to-GDP percentage (with a minimum denominator of 15 percentage points).

Outcome = w × (HDI × 100) + (1 − w) × GE score
GTPI = Outcome / max(Tax-to-GDP %, 15)

Default: 50% HDI / 50% GE. A higher result indicates higher outcomes relative to collected taxes under this model. The 15% floor is an inherited design choice, not a measured threshold.

Classic, recalculated

For a comparison with the original design, use the original CSV's archived HDI, archived governance estimate, and tax-to-GDP. Recalculate rather than trusting the stored scores or ranks, which contain inconsistencies.

Classic = (Original HDI + Original GE estimate)
÷ max(Original Tax-to-GDP % / 100, 0.15)

Classic and preview scores are on different scales and should never be compared directly. The historic estimate scale can produce negative classic scores.

What the score does not mean

Taxes collected as a share of GDP are not the same as government spending, or the tax bill of a typical household. HDI reflects income, health and education, including influences beyond government policy. WGI is based on perceptions and estimates.

Countries differ in resources, demographics, economic structures, public services and non-tax revenue. These rankings are useful for asking questions, but do not establish causation or prove that a particular country's government spends more efficiently.

More complete future editions should document tax observation years, harmonize country coverage, assess score sensitivity, and consider spending-based alternatives.
